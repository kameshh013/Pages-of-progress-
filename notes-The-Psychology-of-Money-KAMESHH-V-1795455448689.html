Saving without a specific goal gives you options, flexibility, and the ability to wait for opportunities in an unpredictable world. Long-Term Strategy • You will change. Long-term financial planning is hard because people's goals and desires change drastically over time. Avoid extreme financial commitments so you do not regret your past choices later in life. • Nothing is free. Every financial return demands a price, though it is not always visible on a price tag. The price of successful investing is volatility, fear, doubt, and regret, which you must view as a fee rather than a fine. • You and me. Do not take financial cues from people playing a different game than you. A day trader and a long-term index investor look at the exact same stock price with completely different goals and timelines. • The seduction of pessimism. Pessimism sounds smarter and more plausible than optimism because setbacks happen fast while progress happens too slowly to notice. In the long run, economic growth and problem-solving usually win out.
